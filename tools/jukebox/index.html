MOTHERLOAD JUKEBOX

A bench for the game's synthesised audio. Every sound here is produced by the same src/audio modules the cockpit runs — nothing is a recording, and nothing is a re-creation.

Scope

Spectrum (log frequency) and waveform, tapped off the master bus. Fire a pad and watch where it lives.

Sound board

Every one-shot in the game. Hold Shift while clicking to fire a pad ten times in a row, which is how you hear whether something is too repetitive to survive being heard a hundred times a run.

Machinery — the voices that run

These are not triggered, they are driven. The drill's timbre comes from the block under the bit: hardness raises and tightens the band it grinds in, and an ore opens a narrow resonant peak pitched off its value.

The environment

The beds and the score, driven exactly as main.js drives them. Drag depth and listen to the descent.

Why it turns sinister

The score is a chord chosen by the rock you are inside. As you go down the root walks down, the mode sours and the filter closes. The bar on the right is sensory roughness — a Plomp–Levelt estimate over the first six partials of every chord tone. It is not a mood setting anybody typed in; it is what those intervals actually do to an ear, and it climbs the whole way.

Design a cockpit sound

Layers of the same two primitives everything in the game is built from: tone() (an oscillator with an envelope) and burst() (filtered noise with an envelope). Build something, audition it, then take the code — it drops into src/audio/audio.js as-is.
